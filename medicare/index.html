Medigap or Medicare Advantage?

Medigap costs more up front but stays flat no matter how much care you use. Medicare Advantage often costs less up front but grows with your care, up to a yearly cap. The honest question isn't which one is "better" — it's how much care you'd need to use in a year before Medigap's steadier cost wins out, and what your state's rules mean for that premium over time and your right to switch later.

What this does and doesn't account for

The breakeven counts your own quoted premiums against a standard 20% Medicare coinsurance rate as a simplified stand-in for how Medicare Advantage cost-sharing typically works — real plans mix flat copays and coinsurance that vary by service and by specific plan, so treat the exact dollar breakeven as a useful estimate, not a guarantee. Medigap's own plan-letter differences (for example, Plan N's small office-visit copays) aren't modeled at this level of detail either.

It does not model prescription drug coverage on the Medicare Advantage side (most Advantage plans bundle it into the numbers already being compared), the extra benefits many Advantage plans include (dental, vision, hearing), network restrictions, or guaranteed-issue rights tied to an employer changing your retiree coverage — a real, separate protection in roughly two dozen states that isn't modeled here yet.
